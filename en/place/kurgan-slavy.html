Mound of Glory

Monument to the liberation of Belarus

A 35-metre mound on the Minsk–Moscow highway crowned by four bayonet-shaped obelisks. A symbol of the liberation of Belarus in 1944.

The Mound of Glory is a memorial complex at the 21st kilometre of the Minsk–Moscow highway. It is dedicated to the liberation of Belarus in 1944.

The 35-metre mound is crowned by four concrete bayonet-shaped obelisks, each 35.6 metres high and clad in titanium. They symbolise the four fronts that took part in the Belarusian operation.

The mound was founded on 30 September 1966 by representatives of hero cities, military units, workers and students of Minsk and the Minsk region. It opened on 5 July 1969.

The memorial was created by sculptor Andrei Bembel, architect Arkady Stakhovich and engineer Vladimir Laptsevich. Today it is one of the most visited places of military memory in Belarus.
