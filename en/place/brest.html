Brest Fortress

Hero fortress on the western border

A 19th-century fortress that became one of the most stubborn centres of resistance in June 1941. Since 1965 it has been a “Hero Fortress”.

Brest Fortress was built in 1830–1842 as a first-class fortress on the western border of the Russian Empire. It consists of four fortifications: the Citadel, Kobrin, Volyn and Terespol.

In June 1941 the fortress became one of the most stubborn centres of resistance. The encircled garrison lacked ammunition, medicine, food and water; the last pockets of resistance held out until late July.

On 8 May 1965 the fortress was awarded the title “Hero Fortress”. The memorial complex opened on 25 September 1971 and covers about 70 hectares.

Today the site combines ruins and surviving structures, sculptures and museum exhibitions: the Defence Museum, “The Museum of War — Territory of Peace”, “The Chronicle of Brest Fortress”, “The Defence of the Eastern Fort” and the “Museum of Fort 5” branch.
