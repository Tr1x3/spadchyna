Khatyn

Memorial on the site of a burned village

Memorial1943 · memorial since 1969Minsk Region

Status

A village burned together with its inhabitants on 22 March 1943. Today Khatyn is one of the main symbols of memory for hundreds of destroyed Belarusian villages.

Khatyn is first mentioned in written sources in the mid-16th century. Before the war it was a small village in the Lahoysk district whose residents worked in agriculture.

On 22 March 1943 a punitive battalion surrounded the village. People were driven into a collective-farm barn and burned; 149 residents died in the fire, including 75 children. The village was completely destroyed.

The memorial complex opened in 1969. Its authors were architects Yu. Gradov, V. Zankovich, L. Levin and sculptor S. Selikhanov. The layout follows the structure of the former village: the Cemetery of Villages, the Square of Memory, the Wall of Sorrow and the Tree of Life.

The central sculpture “The Unconquered Man” depicts Khatyn resident Iosif Kaminsky holding his murdered son. The memorial became a symbol of memory for hundreds of Belarusian villages that shared Khatyn’s fate.
